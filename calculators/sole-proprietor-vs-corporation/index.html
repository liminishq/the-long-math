Sole Proprietor vs Corporation Calculator

Should you earn business income personally or through a corporation? This calculator compares the after-tax capital available to invest under each structure, using your income, spending need, RRSP room, province, and corporate tax assumptions.

The main question is not simply “which structure pays less tax today?” The better question is: after you fund your lifestyle, how much capital is left to compound?

For a sole proprietor, RRSP contributions can reduce income at personal marginal tax rates and create a refund that can be reinvested. For a corporation, active business income may be taxed at a lower initial corporate rate, allowing more after-tax capital to remain inside the corporation if you do not need to spend it personally.

This calculator compares those two starting investment bases.

What this calculator shows

  • How much gets invested through the personal / RRSP path
  • How much can be retained and invested through the corporation
  • Which structure leaves more capital compounding this year
  • How sensitive the result is to RRSP room, income, spending need, and corporate tax assumptions

What this calculator does not do

  • It does not replace professional tax or legal advice
  • It does not model every integration detail perfectly in simple mode
  • It does not automatically decide whether incorporation is worth the legal / accounting complexity
  • It does not account for liability, payroll, GST/HST, professional corporation rules, or compliance costs unless fields are added later

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a corporation always better at high income?

No. It depends on how much income you need personally, how much can be retained, corporate costs, future withdrawal taxes, and investment horizon.

Is RRSP better than incorporating?

They solve different problems. RRSPs defer personal tax to retirement. Corporations can defer personal tax while retaining earnings inside a corporate structure.

Why does RRSP sometimes beat a corporation?

Because an RRSP contribution may be deducted at a high personal marginal tax rate and the refund can be reinvested, creating a larger starting investment base.

Why does a corporation sometimes beat RRSP?

Because RRSP room is capped. At high income with large surplus earnings, a corporation may retain more capital after the small business tax than the individual could invest after personal tax.

Does this include final withdrawal tax?

The default view focuses on first-year invested capital. Optional projection assumptions may estimate future values, but final withdrawal tax depends on future income, tax rates, and timing.
