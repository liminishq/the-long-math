RRSP Deduction Timing Calculator: Claim Now or Save It for Later?

Compare the arithmetic of claiming an RRSP deduction this year versus saving the deduction for a future higher-income year. The calculator uses The Long Math’s shared Canadian income tax engine and the shared investment growth engine.

The question is not “which year gives the biggest refund?” The question is whether the future refund beats what today’s refund could earn, pay down, or otherwise do during the waiting period.

No opinions. No hidden assumptions. Just arithmetic.

    Scope and Assumptions
    
      
      
        
    • Employment income path. The shared engine includes federal/provincial brackets, non-refundable credits (including the federal enhanced BPA phase-out), Ontario surtax, Ontario Tax Reduction (basic amount), and Ontario Health Premium where applicable.
      • 
        
    • Income-tested benefits and clawbacks (CCB, GST/HST credit, OAS recovery tax, GIS, and similar programs) are not income tax and are not modelled. The comparison is tax-only.
      • 
        
    • Future income is entered in that future year’s dollars. When official tax tables for the future year are unavailable, indexed thresholds are projected mechanically from the latest official year using the inflation input. Rates and non-indexed parameters stay unchanged. This is not a forecast of future tax legislation.
      • 
        
    • The RRSP contribution is assumed to have already been made or be otherwise available to deduct. This calculator compares deduction timing, not contribution room.
      • 
        
    • The tax saving is the change in federal plus provincial or territorial income tax. CPP and EI are excluded from the refund comparison because an RRSP deduction does not reduce payroll contributions.
      • 
        
    • The opportunity-cost rate should be the return or interest saving you actually retain after fees and investment taxes, or the debt interest avoided.
      • 
        
    • All strategy values are compared at the future date in nominal dollars.

    FAQ
    
      
    
        Should I claim my RRSP deduction now or save it for later?
        
    Claim now when the compounded value of today’s refund is larger than the future tax saving. Save the deduction only when the higher-income year is likely, close, and large enough to beat that opportunity cost.
    
      
    
      
    
        Is the marginal tax rate enough for this decision?
        
    No. The calculator uses the full tax difference before and after the deduction. That produces a blended deduction rate across the whole RRSP deduction, which matters when the deduction crosses more than one bracket.
    
      
    
      
    
        Does this calculate my official CRA refund?
        
    No. It estimates the tax difference created by the RRSP deduction under explicit assumptions. Your actual refund also depends on withholding, credits, other deductions, benefits, and other tax-return details.
